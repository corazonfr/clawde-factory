## Script
| Beat | Voice-over | Picture (never describe what the viewer already sees) |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | In 2013, a London skyscraper melted a car. Without touching it. | Golden glint off a curved glass tower; a beam lands on a parked car; white flash |
| Setting | London. The City's shiny new skyscraper: thirty-seven floors, nicknamed the Walkie-Talkie. | Day. Painted City street, old stone buildings, the new tower bulging above them; proud office workers |
| Action 1 | One sunny afternoon, a man parked his Jaguar on the street below. An hour later, the wing mirror had melted. So had the Jaguar badge. | Businessman locks a sleek dark car, walks off; time skip; mirror droops like cheese, badge drips (KICK) |
| Action 2 | A shop's doormat got scorched. Paving tiles cracked. | Shopkeeper sees smoke curling off a doormat; crack zigzags across a paving slab |
| Action 3 | A reporter brought a frying pan and fried an egg on the pavement. Then he ate it. For lunch. | Reporter with notepad and pan crouches in the bright patch; egg sizzles; he eats it with a fork |
| Action 4 | The street was hitting over ninety degrees. Celsius. | Thermometer on a lamppost shoots up; pigeons move into the shade |
| Snap | The newspapers renamed it the Walkie-Scorchie. | Newspaper slaps onto the pavement (picture only, no readable headline text) |
| Deadpan double | Luckily, it only happens when the sun shines, and this is London. Unluckily, that summer was the sunniest in seven years. | Grey clouds roll in, everyone relaxes; clouds part, blazing sun, everyone flinches (KICK) |
| Explanation | A flat window bounces sunlight straight back. But this whole front curves inward, like the inside of a spoon. And a curve like that sends all the sunlight it catches to one spot. Up to six times brighter than the sun. | Kid in a park holding a shiny spoon: flat side, nothing; curved side, a bright dot lands on an ice cream, which melts; then aerial of the curved facade with beams converging on the street |
| Consequence | The developers paid nine hundred and forty-six pounds to fix the car. Then they bolted giant metal sunshades across the whole front. The original design had sunshades. They were cut, to save money. | Cheque handed over; cranes lift long metal fins onto the facade; flashback card of the drawing with fins being crossed out |
| Callback twist | The architect said he knew it would reflect. He just didn't realise it would be so hot. Odd, because three years earlier, his hotel in Las Vegas was accused of scorching sunbathers by its pool. So it wasn't a mistake. It was a sequel. | Architect caricature (our own, unnamed) shrugs; cut to a desert pool deck, a curved hotel, sunbathers leaping up; back to London, final push-in on the finned tower glinting |

Word count: 236, about 64 s at 3.7 words/s (limit 70 s). Trim the doormat or spoon line if the voice runs long.
