## Facts and sources
| Fact used | Source |
|---|---|
| Construction began in 1173; the tower began to lean around 1178, once the third floor was built, on a foundation only about 3 m deep in soft subsoil | [History Hit](https://www.historyhit.com/locations/leaning-tower-of-pisa/), [Mental Floss](https://mentalfloss.com/article/70395/13-straight-facts-about-leaning-tower-pisa) |
| Wars between Italian city-states stopped work for almost a century (resumed 1272); the pause let the soil settle and probably prevented collapse | [Grunge](https://www.grunge.com/319881/the-real-reason-it-took-200-years-to-finish-the-leaning-tower-of-pisa), [The Collector](https://www.thecollector.com/leaning-tower-pisa/) |
| Later builders made the upper floors taller on the leaning side to compensate, so the tower is slightly curved | [Mental Floss](https://mentalfloss.com/article/70395/13-straight-facts-about-leaning-tower-pisa) |
| 1838: a walkway (the "catino") was dug around the base; groundwater flooded it and the lean increased | [Ingenia (Burland)](https://ingenia.org.uk/articles/the-stabilisation-of-the-leaning-tower-of-pisa/) |
| 1934: Mussolini, embarrassed by the tilt, had hundreds of holes drilled (361) and tons of grout pumped into the foundation; it leaned further | [Mental Floss](https://www.mentalfloss.com/culture/architecture/13-straight-facts-about-leaning-tower-pisa), [History.com](https://www.history.com/this-day-in-history/february-27/leaning-tower-needs-help) |
| Closed in 1990; soil removed in small amounts from under the north (high) side; reopened 2001; lean reduced by roughly 40 cm (sources give 38-48 cm); never meant to be straight | [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/question/What-efforts-have-been-made-to-ensure-the-Leaning-Tower-of-Pisa-stays-up), [ICE](https://www.ice.org.uk/what-is-civil-engineering/what-do-civil-engineers-do/stabilising-the-leaning-tower-of-pisa/), [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/leaning-tower-of-pisa-corrects-itself-a-little/) |

Avoided on purpose: exact grout tonnage and final angle (sources disagree), and Galileo's dropped balls (probably a legend).

## Script
| Beat | Voice-over | Picture (never describe what the viewer already sees) |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | The most famous mistake in the world was saved by a war. | Golden-hour push-in on the white tower, leaning; white flash |
| Setting | Pisa, 1173. A rich, proud city, building the most beautiful bell tower in Italy. | Day. Painted Tuscan square, masons hauling marble, a proud mayor |
| Action 1 | Three floors up, it started to tip. | Builders on scaffold freeze; a bucket slides along a plank |
| Action 2 | The ground underneath was soft, wet clay, and the foundation was just three metres deep. | Cutaway below ground: shallow ring footing on squishy clay |
| Action 3 | Then war broke out. Everyone dropped their tools and left. For almost a hundred years. | Tools clatter down; crowd marches off; grass and seasons over the stump |
| Snap | Which was the best thing that could have happened to it. | Hold on the abandoned stump, one pigeon lands |
| Explanation | Wet clay is like a sponge. Squeeze it fast, and it squishes. Squeeze it slowly, and the water seeps out, and it gets firm. For a century, the tower squeezed. Slowly. | Kid squeezing a sponge at a fountain, fast then slow; drops seeping from clay under the tower |
| Escalation | In 1272, the builders came back, and made the new floors taller on the low side, to bend it straight. So now it doesn't just lean. It curves. Like a banana. | Masons stacking a wedge-shaped floor; tower gains a gentle curve; banana cart in foreground |
| Deadpan double | In 1838, an architect dug a walkway around the base so visitors could see the bottom. Luckily, it looked great. Unluckily, it filled with water. | Proud architect with a lantern; trench fills; the tower dips a notch (KICK) |
| Escalation 2 | In 1934, Mussolini decided a leaning tower was embarrassing. He drilled hundreds of holes and pumped in tons of cement. It leaned more. | Stern dictator figure (unnamed caricature, no symbols), drills, cement pump; tower dips again (KICK) |
| Consequence | By 1990, it was so close to falling that they shut it. So engineers did the opposite of everyone before them. Instead of pushing it up, they took a little soil out from under the high side, one bucket at a time, and let it sink back. | Gates close; engineers with laptops and thin augers; tiny buckets of soil; tower eases back |
| Callback twist | Eleven years later, it had straightened by about forty centimetres. And then they stopped. On purpose. Because a straight tower in Pisa is just a tower. | Golden hour again, tourists doing the "holding it up" pose; final push-in |

Word count: about 240 (target 210-250).
