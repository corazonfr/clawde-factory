# The Jet That Ran Out of Fuel Because of a Unit Mix-Up

Cartoon 3D short (skill: `cartoon-3d-short`). Vertical 1080x1920 (final 1440x2560), target ~70 s.
Status: written and produced by the daily scheduled run (2026-11-24).

## Facts and sources
| Fact used | Source |
|---|---|
| 23 July 1983, Air Canada Flight 143, a Boeing 767, ran out of fuel at 41,000 ft over Red Lake, Ontario | [Wikipedia: Gimli Glider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gimli_Glider), [CBC Archives](https://www.cbc.ca/archives/when-a-metric-mix-up-led-to-the-gimli-glider-emergency-1.4754039) |
| The 767 was new to the fleet and all-metric (fuel in kilograms); the fuel-quantity gauges were not working, so fuel was calculated by hand | [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gimli_Glider), [View from the Wing](https://viewfromthewing.com/both-engines-died-at-41000-feet-canadas-metric-switch-left-a-brand-new-boeing-767-with-half-the-fuel-it-needed/) |
| The density used was in lb/L instead of kg/L: they loaded 22,300 lb (~10,100 kg) instead of the 22,300 kg needed (less than half) | [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gimli_Glider), [View from the Wing](https://viewfromthewing.com/both-engines-died-at-41000-feet-canadas-metric-switch-left-a-brand-new-boeing-767-with-half-the-fuel-it-needed/) |
| Both engines stopped; the 767's electronic cockpit displays went blank; it glided ~17 minutes | [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gimli_Glider) |
| 61 passengers + 8 crew = 69 people | [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gimli_Glider) |
| The captain was an experienced glider (sailplane) pilot | [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gimli_Glider), [AOPA](https://www.aopa.org/news-and-media/all-news/2000/july/pilot/the-gimli-glider) |
| Could not reach Winnipeg; diverted to the former RCAF Station Gimli, part of which was a race complex with a drag strip; a race was underway that day | [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gimli_Glider) |
| The first officer had served at RCAF Station Gimli | [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gimli_Glider) |
| Too high on approach, the captain used a forward slip (flying sideways) to lose height; the nose gear did not lock and collapsed; the jet stopped on its nose | [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gimli_Glider) |
| No serious injuries (minor ones on the evacuation slides) | [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gimli_Glider) |
| Repaired, flew out two days later, retired in 2008 (~25 years); known as the "Gimli Glider" | [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gimli_Glider) |

Avoided on purpose: the crew's and mechanics' names, the often repeated "mechanics' van ran out of fuel" story (no source
found), the fire in the nose and how it was put out, the exact stopping distance from the crowd, the later scrapping.

## Script
| Beat | Voice-over | Picture |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | In 1983, a brand-new jet ran out of fuel at forty-one thousand feet. Because of a maths mistake. | Jet above clouds, engines sputter; WHITE FLASH |
| Setting | Air Canada had just got its first metric plane, a Boeing 767. It measured fuel in kilograms. The fuel gauge was broken, so they worked it out by hand. But their numbers were in pounds. | Shiny jet at a gate; fuel truck; worker with a clipboard scratching head |
| Escalation 1 | They needed twenty-two thousand three hundred kilos. They loaded twenty-two thousand three hundred pounds. Less than half. | Two fuel tanks side by side: one full, one less than half |
| Escalation 2 | Over Ontario, a warning beeped. Then the left engine stopped. Then the right one. Then most of the cockpit screens went dark. | Cockpit: pilots, a red light; engine puffs and stops; second; screens go black |
| Snap | Sixty-nine people were now riding a very heavy glider. | Silent jet over clouds, passengers' faces in windows |
| Deadpan double | Luckily, the captain flew gliders for fun. Unluckily, they could not reach Winnipeg. The closest runway was an old air force base in Gimli, Manitoba. Part of it was now a race track. And that day, there was a race on. | Captain calm; then the airfield from above: race cars, a crowd, kids on bikes |
| Explanation | Here's the mix-up. A pound is less than half a kilo. It's like a recipe that says two kilos of flour, and you weigh out two pounds. Same number. Less than half a cake. | Kitchen: a scale, a flour bag, a flat sad half-cake |
| Landing | The jet came in too high, so the captain turned it sideways, like a skier braking, and dropped. The nose wheel folded, and the jet slid to a stop on its nose. Nobody was seriously hurt. | Jet slips sideways; skier inset; touchdown, nose drops (KICK), slides; people cheer |
| Callback twist | Why Gimli? The first officer had served at that base years before. And the jet? It was patched up, flew for another twenty-five years, and got a nickname. The Gimli Glider. | Young first officer at the old base; the jet flying again in sun; final push-in |
