## Voice-over (as sent to ElevenLabs)
In 1980, an oil rig drilled one small hole in the bottom of a lake. A few hours later, the lake was gone.
Lake Peigneur, Louisiana. Ten feet deep. Fishing boats, a garden island, and far below the mud, a salt mine full of miners.
On the twentieth of November, the drill got stuck. Then the rig began to tilt. Then the water began to spin. The crew jumped off and ran. The rig went down the hole. Then the barges. Then the trees. Then most of the island.
The drill had punched straight into the mine, and the whole lake followed it down.
Luckily, every miner got out alive. Unluckily, the mine was now the lake.
The problem was salt. Imagine drinking through a straw made of sugar. Every sip makes the straw a little wider, so the next sip is bigger. The fresh water ate the salt, the hole grew, and the water rushed in faster.
It drained so hard that the canal to the sea ran backwards, and for a few days Louisiana had the tallest waterfall in its history.
Days later, nine of the barges popped back up like corks. The lake filled with seawater, and is now salty and two hundred feet deep.
They drilled for oil. They found a plughole.
