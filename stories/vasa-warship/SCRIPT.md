## Voice-over (as sent to ElevenLabs)
In 1628, Sweden built one of the mightiest warships in the world. It sank before it left the harbour.
Stockholm, the tenth of August. Crowds lined the shore. She carried two full decks of heavy cannon.
The ship fired a salute. The sails filled. A small gust tipped her over. She came back up. A bigger gust tipped her further.
Then water poured in through the open gunports, and less than half an hour into her first voyage, just over a kilometre from the dock, she sank.
Luckily, they had tested her stability before she sailed. Unluckily, the test was thirty men running back and forth across the deck. She rocked so hard they stopped the test. Then they sailed anyway.
Think of a bookcase with all the heavy books on the top shelf. It stands perfectly still, until somebody leans on it. Vasa had too much ship above the water and not enough weight below it.
There was an inquiry. Nobody was punished.
But the Baltic is too fresh for the worms that eat wooden ships. So she lay in the mud for three hundred and thirty-three years, was lifted out in 1961, and is now the most visited museum in Scandinavia.
The ship that couldn't stay up for half an hour has been standing up for sixty-five years.
