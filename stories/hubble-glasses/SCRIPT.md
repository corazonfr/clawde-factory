# The Space Telescope That Needed Glasses

Cartoon 3D short (skill: `cartoon-3d-short`). Vertical 1080x1920 (final 1440x2560), target ~70 s.
Status: written and produced by the daily scheduled run (2026-11-25).

## Facts and sources
| Fact used | Source |
|---|---|
| Hubble launched 24 April 1990 aboard Space Shuttle Discovery (STS-31) | [NASA: Hubble's mirror flaw](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/hubble/overview/hubbles-mirror-flaw), [ESA/Hubble: the aberration problem](https://esahubble.org/about/history/aberration_problem/) |
| It cost about $1.5 billion at launch (some sources say ~$1.6 billion, so the script says "one and a half billion") | [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/an-ingenius-fix-for-hubbles-famously-flawed-vision), [SpaceNews](https://spacenews.com/?p=9925) |
| The first images were blurry; the flaw was announced on 27 June 1990, two months after launch | [NASA](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/hubble/overview/hubbles-mirror-flaw) |
| The 2.4 m primary mirror was ground to the wrong shape: too flat towards the edge (spherical aberration), so light did not come to one focus | [NASA](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/hubble/overview/hubbles-mirror-flaw), [ESA/Hubble](https://esahubble.org/about/history/aberration_problem/) |
| The error was less than 1/50 of the thickness of a human hair | [Smithsonian NASM: Repairing Hubble](https://www.airandspace.si.edu/stories/editorial/repairing-hubble) |
| The mirror was one of the most precisely figured mirrors ever made, smooth to about 10 nm, but to the wrong shape | [Wikipedia: Hubble Space Telescope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubble_Space_Telescope) |
| Cause: a lens in the null corrector, the test device that guided the polishing, was spaced 1.3 mm wrong | [NASA](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/hubble/overview/hubbles-mirror-flaw) |
| Fix: COSTAR, a phone-booth-sized instrument with pairs of small corrective mirrors, worked "like a pair of eyeglasses" | [NASA](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/hubble/overview/hubbles-mirror-flaw), [Wikipedia: COSTAR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corrective_Optics_Space_Telescope_Axial_Replacement) |
| Installed by the crew of Endeavour (STS-61) in December 1993, over five spacewalks; images came back sharp | [NASA: Servicing Mission 1](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/hubble/observatory/missions-to-hubble/servicing-mission-1), [ESA/Hubble: SM1](https://esahubble.org/about/history/servicing_mission_1/) |
| Every instrument installed after the flaw was found has its own built-in correction | [NASA](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/hubble/overview/hubbles-mirror-flaw) |
| Kodak made a backup mirror with different test equipment; it does not have the flaw, never flew, and is on display at the National Air and Space Museum in Washington | [Smithsonian: backup mirror](https://si.edu/object/mirror-primary-backup-hubble-space-telescope:nasm_A20010288000), [Smithsonian NASM](https://www.airandspace.si.edu/stories/editorial/repairing-hubble) |

Avoided on purpose: anyone's name, the disputed "washer" story of how the lens got out of place, the 2 vs 2.2 micron
figure (sources differ, so the hair comparison is used), late-night TV jokes about the telescope.

## Script (~225 words)
| Beat | Voice-over | Picture |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | NASA spent one and a half billion dollars on a space telescope. Then it turned out to need glasses. | Hubble in orbit over the Earth, a star field; the telescope "squints"; WHITE FLASH |
| Setting | April 1990. The shuttle Discovery carries Hubble above the atmosphere, where the stars should finally look sharp. | Shuttle launch from the pad, smoke; then Hubble released over the Earth |
| Escalation | The first pictures arrive. They're blurry. The engineers adjust the focus. Still blurry. They adjust it again. Two months later, NASA explains why. | Control room: screen with a fuzzy blob; engineers lean in, turn a knob; blob stays fuzzy; one engineer puts their head on the desk |
| Snap | The main mirror, two point four metres wide, was the wrong shape. Its edge was too flat, by less than one fiftieth of the width of a human hair. | Big mirror on a stand in a clean lab; a hair next to it |
| Deadpan double | Luckily, it had been polished very carefully. Unluckily, it was polished carefully to the wrong shape. The tool that checked it had one lens sitting one point three millimetres out of place. | Technician polishing; test tool with a lens a little off |
| Explanation | It's the same problem as bad eyesight. Light from the edge and light from the middle land in different places, so nothing is ever quite in focus. And the fix is the same, too. | Eye chart, a cartoon kid squinting; then glasses |
| Consequence | In 1993, astronauts on Endeavour caught Hubble, and over five spacewalks fitted a box the size of a phone booth, full of small corrective mirrors. A pair of glasses for a telescope. The pictures came back sharp. | Shuttle arm grabs Hubble; astronauts on spacewalk carry a box in; sharp galaxy on the control-room screen; engineers cheer |
| Callback twist | And the backup mirror? A different company built it, with different test equipment. It had the right shape all along. It never flew. It's in a museum in Washington. | Museum hall, the backup mirror behind a rope; a kid looks at it; final push-in |
