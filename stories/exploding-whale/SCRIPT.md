Cartoon 3D short (skill: `cartoon-3d-short`). Vertical 1080x1920 (final 1440x2560), target 55-70 s.
Status: written and produced end to end on the user's request ("make another one and post it when you're done").

## Script
| Beat | Voice-over | Picture |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | In 1970, Oregon tried to get rid of a dead whale. With half a ton of dynamite. | Grey beach, a huge whale; a plunger box; WHITE FLASH |
| Setting | Florence, Oregon. A forty-five-foot sperm whale had washed up on the beach. Eight tons, and getting smellier every day. | Wide beach, dunes, grey Pacific; the whale; locals hold their noses, stink lines |
| Action 1 | It was the highway department's problem. So they treated it like a boulder in the road. | Highway truck pulls up; engineer with hard hat sizes up the whale like a rock |
| Action 2 | The plan: blow it into tiny pieces, and let the seagulls eat the leftovers. | Engineer points; seagulls on a post look interested |
| Action 3 | A veteran with explosives training said twenty sticks would do it. They used twenty cases. | Veteran holds up a few sticks; workers carry stack after stack of crates (KICK on "cases") |
| Action 4 | Crowds gathered. TV cameras rolled. Everyone moved back a quarter of a mile. | Crowd on the dunes, TV camera on a tripod, everyone shuffles far back |
| Snap | Then they pushed the button. | Plunger pushed; BOOM, sand and smoke column (KICK) |
| Deadpan double | Luckily, nobody was hurt. Unluckily, a quarter of a mile was not far enough. Chunks of whale rained down on the crowd. One crushed the roof of a brand-new car. | Crowd cheers; then looks up; blubber rains, people run; a chunk lands on a shiny car (KICK) |
| Gulls | And the seagulls? The blast scared them all away. | Gulls flee off screen |
| Explanation | Here's the problem. Blubber is thick, rubbery fat. Dynamite doesn't turn it into dust. It just throws it. Stomp on a ketchup packet, and you don't get a fine mist. You get big red splats. Everywhere. | Picnic: a kid stomps a ketchup packet; splats land on dad; back to the beach, chunks everywhere |
| Consequence | Highway crews bulldozed and buried the rest. Today, Oregon just buries beached whales where they land. | Bulldozer pushes sand; later, a whale-shaped mound of sand |
| Callback twist | Oh, and that crushed car? Its owner had bought it at a sale promising a whale of a deal. And fifty years later, Florence voted to name a new park. The winner: Exploding Whale Memorial Park. | Flashback: dealership with a whale banner (no text); then sunny modern park, a sign with a whale picture and a little blast; final push-in |
